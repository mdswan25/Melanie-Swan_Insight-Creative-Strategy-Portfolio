The Approach · Overview

Find the reason people buy, write the brief around it, and let the results shape the next one.

Every case below follows the same loop. I start with research (customer interviews, purchase behavior, competitive scans, brand tracking) and narrow it to one insight. That insight becomes a brief that gives creative, design, and content teams clear direction. After launch, A/B and holdout results become the research for the next round. AI tools speed up the research, ideation, and drafting, and I make the final call on creative direction.

Case Study 01

Replacing Discounts With a Reason to Stay

Global Consumer Products · Premium Haircare

20% → 25%

The Insight

Competitive and customer research found underserved segments that a discount-led model wasn't reaching. The answer was a better reason to stay, not a bigger discount.

Research

The salon-professional loyalty program had lost relevance, and its budget wasn't performing. I led competitive analysis and customer research to find which segments the program was missing and why.

Creative Direction

I rebuilt the value proposition around a non-discount tiered model built on access and experience. I wrote the briefs and directed 3–4 agency partners on campaigns, photo shoots, webpages, and trade show displays.

Testing & Learning

We tested headlines, imagery, value propositions, offers, and CTAs, and moved budget from legacy spend to what the tests proved. The program later expanded into EMEA.

The Key Decision

"One engine, five front doors": a shared segmentation and insight backbone, with each brand's creative team keeping its own voice, look, and rewards. Leadership adopted and funded it.

Research

Five restaurant brands ran customer marketing separately, so every team started from a blank page. Customer and co-marketing analysis showed the brands could share segmentation and lifecycle logic while still speaking in their own voices.

Creative Direction

I turned purchase-behavior and segment insights into messaging angles and offers across the D2C app, online ordering, in-app, and email. I made the case to the CMO for AI-driven personalization, and it was funded.

Partnerships

I coordinated co-branded partnerships with JetBlue and Fandango, ran bonus-points campaigns, and reviewed partner performance quarterly with VP leadership.

Two smaller brands pushed back. I slowed their rollout by a quarter and co-designed brand-specific rewards with them instead of forcing a launch.

~40%

The Key Decision

Build forecasting, segmentation, and testing before the pilot, so every message could be measured. Leadership funded it.

Research

There was no loyalty or referral program. Member interviews and journey mapping showed where enrollment stalled. A tenure-based segmentation didn't match who the best members were, so I rebuilt it around recency and referral behavior.

Creative Direction

I briefed newsletters, member advocacy stories, and a ~200-member referral pilot, and rewrote complex benefits as plain-language messaging and call center scripts. All of it passed legal and compliance review.

How AI Fit In

I used generative AI for research synthesis, copy ideation, message variations, brief structure, and first drafts, and Glean to find past briefs and approvals. A person reviewed every output for voice, accuracy, and compliance.

Case Study 04

Letting Test Results Write the Next Brief

Telecommunications · Residential & Small Business
+16%
Conversion vs. prior cycle
+22%
Digital engagement
The Key Decision

Move from static lists to behavioral segmentation, and validate every message and offer against a holdout group.

Research

Campaigns ran on static, rule-based lists with manual QA, so they were slow to launch and hard to learn from. Real-time data was limited, so the plan had to work within the existing stack.

Creative Direction

I scoped automation and personalization with CRM and engineering teams, redesigned omnichannel journeys, and briefed creative partners on webpages, collateral, and direct mail.

Testing & Learning

I shared results monthly with senior leadership and quarterly with the CMO, and used them to set the next cycle's priorities.

The first automated triggers sent too often and opt-outs spiked. I rebuilt the frequency and suppression rules to reduce unsubscribes and keep the engagement gains.

The Brief Framework

How I give creative, design, and content teams clear direction.

I use the same brief structure across campaigns, channels, and industries, whether the work goes to an agency, an in-house studio, or an AI-assisted production team. Each field answers one question the team would otherwise have to guess at.

Creative Brief · Field GuideEvergreen Format
Objective
What should the customer do, and what business result does that move? One measurable goal per brief.
Audience
Who we're talking to, described by behavior and purchase motivation, not only demographics.
Insight
The one customer need or tension, drawn from research, that explains why people buy or hesitate.
Proposition
The single-minded thing we want the customer to believe, written in one sentence.
Reasons to Believe
The product benefits, proof points, and policies that make the proposition credible. Only claims we can stand behind.
Angles to Test
Two or three distinct messaging angles, each tied back to the insight, so testing tells us something useful.
Script & Copy Direction

Mandatories

Deliverables

Measure & Learn
The metrics that define success for each angle, and how the results will shape the next brief.
The Scorecard I Use for Briefs & Agencies
  • Strategic alignment. Does it serve the business goal?
  • Audience insight. Is it built on a real customer need?
  • Creative quality. Is it distinctive and on brand?
  • Brief adherence. Did the work answer the brief?
  • Execution. On time, on budget, and accurate?
  • Results. What did the numbers say?
  • Collaboration. How well did the teams work together?
AI in My Workflow

  • Ideation. Messaging variations and hooks for each angle.
  • Briefs & scripts. Structure and first drafts, then rewritten for voice.
  • Operations. Meeting recaps, action items, and finding past briefs and approvals.
  • Judgment. I challenge the outputs. The final creative direction is my call.
